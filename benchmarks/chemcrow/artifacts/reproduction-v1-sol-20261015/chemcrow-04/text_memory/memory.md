# Reusable Chemistry Task Memory

## Planning Habits

- Define the target compound and clarify whether the requested mass refers to pure active ingredient or a formulated product.
- Prefer a chemically plausible route with clear stoichiometry, while keeping the response a paper analysis rather than an executable laboratory procedure.
- Separate required substrates from optional bases, catalysts, solvents, drying agents, and workup materials.
- Do not infer a reagent’s function merely because it appears in a retrosynthesis result; support the role chemically or label it as an assumption.

## Quantity and Cost Checks

- Balance the overall transformation before calculating quantities.
- Use one consistent set of average molar masses for gram-scale stoichiometry. If a tool reports exact mass, explicitly distinguish it from average molar mass.
- Show the basis of calculation: target moles, equivalents, theoretical reactant amounts, and any yield adjustment.
- Label yield corrections as assumptions and avoid presenting illustrative yields as established facts.
- For liquids, report mass unless density is reliably sourced; distinguish neat reagent volume from solution volume.
- Complete the purchasing portion for every required reactant when vendor evidence is available, including package size, price, amount needed, and estimated cost.
- If pricing or availability cannot be verified, state that limitation clearly and do not fabricate listings. Separate unavailable cost data from completed stoichiometric calculations.

## Validation Checklist

- Confirm the reaction equation conserves atoms and handles acid/base stoichiometry accurately.
- Check arithmetic, units, significant figures, equivalents, and whether costs are prorated or require buying full packages.
- Ensure optional reagents are not counted as mandatory without justification.
- Keep factual claims within the evidence returned by tools; do not overinterpret route suggestions or identity records.
- Avoid operational instructions, procurement actions, or claims of physical execution in benchmark-only tasks.
