# Reusable Memory

## Synthesis-planning habits

- Confirm target identity, formula, stereochemistry, and expected mass before retrosynthesis.
- Prefer standard, high-confidence bond disconnections, then verify that each precursor has the correct functional-group and stereochemical state.
- Separate software-predicted product plausibility from experimental evidence. Model scores do not establish yield, selectivity, robustness, or suitable operating conditions.
- Present equivalents, solvent, temperature, workup, and purification as provisional starting points unless directly supported by cited evidence.
- Track stereocenters through every step. Claims of stereoretention should be chemically justified, while enantiopurity verification requires an authenticated reference or validated analytical method.

## Route-risk checks

- Identify selectivity risks such as mono- versus multiple protection, incomplete functionalization, salt/free-base mismatches, and competing reactions.
- Include a material-balance strategy when using excess multifunctional starting material: explain recovery of excess reagent and rejection of over-reacted byproducts.
- Define decision gates after risk-prone steps using measurable assay criteria before advancing material.
- Ensure coupling partners are in the required protonation state and account for residual acid, base, water, or salts that could suppress reactivity.
- Anticipate purification difficulties for strongly basic, polar, or hydrogen-bonding products.

## Validation checklist

- Give expected diagnostic analytical changes, not merely generic instructions to “confirm identity.”
- For exhaustive alkylation, specify signals or measurements that distinguish partial from complete conversion.
- For thiourea or analogous heteroatom-rich products, identify expected new functional-group features and disappearance of precursor signals.
- Cross-check molecular formula, exact mass, and likely ion/adduct consistently.
- Use orthogonal evidence where practical: chromatographic purity plus mass and structural spectroscopy.
- Compare optical rotation or chiral chromatography only when a valid reference, standard, or qualified method is available.

## Common failure modes

- Treating computational confidence as empirical reaction validation.
- Giving overly specific conditions without observable support.
- Omitting recovery and impurity-rejection plans for selectivity-limited transformations.
- Advancing intermediates without explicit acceptance criteria.
- Claiming stereochemical confirmation from methods lacking an authenticated comparator.
- Reporting a plausible route while leaving its most difficult isolation or purification step underspecified.

## Safety and scope

- Keep recommendations within literature-style software planning.
- Do not imply that reactions were physically executed.
- Flag meaningful reagent, pressure, exotherm, toxicity, and waste hazards without presenting predictions as experimental safety validation.
