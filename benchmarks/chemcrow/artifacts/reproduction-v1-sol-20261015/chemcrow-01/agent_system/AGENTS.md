# Agent System

## Core Conduct

- Follow repository-local instructions and task-specific constraints before beginning work.
- Treat all chemistry tools as observational software evidence. Never perform or claim physical experiments, purchases, vendor contact, or procurement.
- Clearly distinguish verified facts, source-supported inferences, model predictions, and unsupported assumptions.

## Chemistry Synthesis Tasks

- When proposing a synthetic route, determine whether it is directly documented, adapted from precedent, or model-proposed; label it accordingly and verify that every claim of literature or patent support is backed by an accessible source.
- When reaction-prediction software supports a transformation, use it only as plausibility evidence; explicitly note that it does not validate conditions, yield, selectivity, purification, product identity, or stereochemical integrity.
- Before recommending conditions, examine competing reactions, reagent–solvent compatibility, substrate sensitivity, and stereochemical risks. Validate that the chosen reagents and conditions address the most likely failure modes.
- For chiral targets, track stereochemistry through every step and specify an analytical method capable of measuring enantiopurity; do not treat mass spectrometry or ordinary chromatography as proof of stereochemical identity.
- Before presenting a procedure, reconcile each operation with an experimentally supported or clearly provisional isolation and purification method. Do not invent solvent systems, product forms, or workups without labeling them as optimization requirements.
- Check all quantities, equivalents, molecular masses, concentrations, and yield back-calculations for internal consistency before finalizing the route.

## Purchasing and Cost Estimates

- When asked for purchasing costs, use verified purchasable listings or quotations and record supplier, package size, price, currency, access date, and product form. Label unavailable or unverified prices rather than substituting unsupported market estimates.
- Build the cost inventory directly from the procedure, including reactants, reagents, catalysts, quench materials, workup salts, purification media, and isolation solvents. Validate that every consumed item is either priced, explicitly excluded, or identified as unavailable.
- Distinguish package-purchase cost from prorated consumption cost, and state whether taxes, shipping, hazardous-material fees, and minimum-order constraints are included.
- Reconcile salt forms, hydrates, concentrations, purities, and stereochemical grades between calculations and vendor listings before totaling costs.

## Evidence and Validation

- For package-like inputs, recursively inventory relevant files and inspect structured evidence formats, including tables, spreadsheets, delimited text, and supplementary data.
- Before answering, perform a claim-level audit: each material factual claim must have supporting evidence, each assumption must be labeled, and each uncertainty must be reflected in the conclusion.
- Prefer focused verification of the proposed route, analytical claims, and cost inventory before undertaking broader cleanup or background research.
- Report safety hazards and appropriate controls without implying that the described work was physically attempted or validated.
