# Chemistry Synthesis and Costing Memory

## Evidence discipline

- Distinguish documented procedures, computational predictions, and chemically plausible proposals.
- Never describe a model-proposed route as literature- or patent-supported unless a primary source establishes the exact transformation and conditions.
- Reaction-prediction tools support plausibility only; they do not validate yield, selectivity, purification, stereochemical integrity, or operational safety.
- Label every unsupported condition, yield, or price as an assumption.

## Route assessment

- Check compound identity, stereochemistry, salt form, and molecular weight before calculating scale.
- For each step, assess competing reactions, reagent–solvent compatibility, substrate stability, and possible racemization.
- Treat reductive aminations cautiously: evaluate direct carbonyl reduction, reagent decomposition, imine-formation conditions, water content, and order of addition.
- Do not compensate for an uncertain reaction merely by proposing large reagent excesses; justify equivalents chemically.
- Require chiral analysis when enantiopurity matters. Mass spectrometry or ordinary chromatography cannot establish stereochemical purity.
- Keep purification and isolation claims conservative unless an experimentally supported solvent system, product form, and workup are available.

## Calculation checks

- State the target form and scale.
- Show the chain from target moles through assumed step yields and reagent equivalents.
- Verify units, molecular masses, limiting reagents, and cumulative-yield back-calculation.
- Separate stoichiometric requirements from practical purchase quantities.

## Purchasing-cost checks

- Use current, verifiable vendor listings when purchasability is requested.
- Record supplier, grade, package size, currency, listing date, and whether the item is actually available.
- Distinguish package purchase cost from prorated material consumption.
- Reconcile the cost inventory with the full procedure, including reagents, quench materials, salts, extraction and chromatography solvents, and crystallization solvents.
- Do not present rough market ranges as quotations. If live pricing cannot be verified, say the purchasing total is incomplete.

## Final validation

- Ensure every claimed route provenance has a supporting source.
- Ensure every procedural item appears in the bill of materials.
- Flag hazardous operations and uncertain chemistry without implying physical execution.
- Present the result as a planning analysis, not as an experimentally validated synthesis.
