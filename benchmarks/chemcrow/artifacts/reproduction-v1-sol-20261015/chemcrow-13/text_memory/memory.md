# Reusable Chemistry Task Memory

## Synthesis planning

- Treat route design as a paper feasibility analysis; never imply physical execution or procurement.
- Prefer documented procedures over merely plausible retrosynthesis.
- For each step, provide stoichiometry, solvent, temperature, time, workup, purification, expected yield, and stereochemical implications.
- Build a complete inventory including catalysts, bases, acids, solvents, drying agents, purification media, and analytical needs.
- Distinguish literature evidence from predictive-tool output. If a model predicts the wrong product or protecting state, do not describe the route as validated.
- Specify salt formation rigorously: stoichiometry, pH, counterion source, hydration state, crystallization, and isolation conditions matter.
- Check whether hydrolysis, deprotection, or lactone-opening steps alter the isolated form of the target.

## Cost analysis

- Give actual price evidence and a numerical total; formulas or qualitative claims alone do not fulfill a costing request.
- Report both consumed-material cost and realistic acquisition cost based on minimum package sizes.
- Include solvents, purification, analysis, waste handling, shipping, and equipment assumptions—or clearly state exclusions.
- Record vendor, package size, purity, currency, and price date because pricing is time-sensitive.
- Compare like with like: research-grade characterized material, pharmaceutical ingredient, salt, free acid, and finished tablets are not interchangeable.
- Separate one-time startup cost from marginal cost per batch.
- Propagate step yields through the route and include a sensitivity range for uncertain yields or prices.

## Validation checklist

- Confirm mass, mole, equivalents, limiting reagent, and cumulative-yield calculations independently.
- Verify every intermediate’s structure, protecting groups, stereochemistry, and final chemical form.
- Reconcile predicted reactions with cited experimental evidence; flag discrepancies instead of smoothing them over.
- Ensure the conclusion follows from the numerical comparison.
- State uncertainty and practical limitations such as scale-up risk, purification losses, characterization burden, and regulatory or safety constraints.
