# Chemistry Synthesis and GHS Reporting

## Scope

Use this skill to prepare an evidence-backed, small-scale synthesis overview and substance-by-substance GHS summary. Treat chemistry tools and references as observational software evidence only. Never claim that a procedure was physically performed, procure materials, or contact vendors.

## Workflow

1. Identify the target compound, requested scale, jurisdiction, and expected level of procedural detail.
2. Locate a reputable synthesis source and confirm:
   - starting materials and transformation;
   - catalyst, solvent, and conditions;
   - workup and purification;
   - reported yield or other outcome.
3. Keep each procedural claim aligned with its source. If substituting a catalyst, solvent, or condition, find separate support and label the change explicitly.
4. Check stoichiometry:
   - convert quantities to moles;
   - identify the limiting reagent;
   - verify equivalents and concentrations;
   - calculate theoretical yield with units and sensible precision.
5. Classify every substance by role:
   - reaction reactant;
   - catalyst or reagent;
   - solvent;
   - quench/workup material;
   - purification material.
6. Retrieve current GHS information from authoritative SDSs or official classification databases. Record the source, jurisdiction, supplier or authority, revision date, concentration or form, and applicable classification.
7. Report GHS as classifications rather than a single “rating”:
   - hazard class and category;
   - pictograms;
   - signal word;
   - hazard statements and codes.
8. Note that classifications can vary with concentration, physical form, supplier, and jurisdiction. Do not infer exact categories or H-codes from general hazard descriptions.
9. Summarize controls that directly follow from the cited hazards, including ventilation, PPE, ignition control, incompatible materials, thermal hazards, and waste handling. Avoid unrelated regulatory commentary.

## Response Structure

- Brief route summary and evidence basis
- Clearly labeled synthesis procedure
- Stoichiometry and theoretical-yield check
- GHS table grouped by substance role
- Concise safety and waste notes
- Sources placed beside the claims they support

Distinguish materials consumed in the reaction from catalysts, solvents, and workup substances. Include auxiliary materials in the hazard table when relevant, but never call them reactants unless they participate in the reaction.

## Verification

Before completion, confirm that:

- the cited route supports the stated transformation and conditions;
- any procedural substitution has independent evidence;
- all quantities, equivalents, and yield calculations are dimensionally correct;
- every listed substance has the correct role;
- exact GHS entries are directly supported by a current, applicable source;
- concentration-dependent classifications are identified;
- reactants are not conflated with workup materials;
- the response contains no claim of physical execution;
- no progress narration, irrelevant controlled-substance discussion, or unsupported certainty remains.
