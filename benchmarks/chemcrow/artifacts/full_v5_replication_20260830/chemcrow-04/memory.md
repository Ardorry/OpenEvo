# Memory

- For chemistry planning tasks, explicitly frame outputs as software-only planning evidence. Do not imply physical execution, procurement, vendor contact, or lab readiness.
- Use the declared chemistry tools for identity, retrosynthesis, molecular weights, CAS lookup, safety/control screening, and purchasability/cost evidence when the task constrains evidence sources.
- When price or vendor lookup tools fail, do not invent costs or imply purchasability. State that the cost portion cannot be completed under the available evidence and optionally provide a placeholder column for vendor-backed prices.
- In stoichiometry tables, label calculated masses as theoretical minimums under stated assumptions, especially 100% yield, purity, and exact equivalents.
- Add a practical caveat that real purchasing quantities may differ because of yield losses, purity, density, reagent excess, solvent/workup needs, and procedure-specific equivalents.
- If simplifying a retrosynthesis output, briefly explain why only the chemically relevant core reactants were retained and why extra predicted species were omitted.
- For chemical tables, include unambiguous identifiers such as CAS numbers when available, but keep hazard/safety notes high level unless the user asks for operational detail.
- Validation checklist: confirm target identity, verify precursor identities, compute molar target amount from molecular weight, multiply by reagent molecular weights and equivalents, mark assumptions, screen safety/control status, and separate verified cost evidence from unavailable or unverified cost data.
