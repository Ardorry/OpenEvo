# Memory

## Reusable Task Habits

- For synthesis-planning prompts, start from a clear retrosynthetic disconnection and identify the key commercial or literature-accessible fragments before discussing optional precursor preparation.
- Treat chemistry tools as software evidence only: report identifiers, structures, and plausibility checks, but never imply physical execution, procurement, or experimental confirmation.
- Keep route narration focused on chemistry, not tool logistics. Mention failed or limited tool outputs only if they affect confidence or route selection.
- Include analytical validation checks such as expected molecular formula/mass, stereochemical assignment, and diagnostic functional-group confirmation.
- When a route uses an enantiopure chiral intermediate, explicitly state how stereochemical integrity is preserved and where racemization or epimerization risk is low or relevant.

## Common Failure Modes

- Avoid overly generic reaction plans. Include enough condition-level detail to distinguish the intended transformation, workup, and purification strategy without becoming a bench protocol.
- Do not leave chiral amine precursor preparation purely conceptual. Address protection/deprotection logic, chemoselectivity, overalkylation risk, and retention of enantiopurity when relevant.
- Safety, patent, or control caveats should influence route choice or handling rationale, not appear only as detached disclaimers.
- Do not overemphasize CAS numbers or identifiers at the expense of mechanistic/selectivity rationale.
- Avoid presenting optional alternative routes as equally good when one direct coupling/disconnection is clearly standard.

## Validation Checklist

- Confirm the target structure from name and SMILES/InChI when available.
- Verify that the chosen nucleophile/electrophile pairing gives the correct unsymmetrical product.
- Check that any tertiary amine or other basic group need not be protected under the proposed coupling conditions, and explain why.
- Confirm stereochemistry of the chiral fragment and ensure no step plausibly scrambles it.
- Include concise expected characterization endpoints rather than claiming observed spectra or yields.
