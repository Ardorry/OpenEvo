# Memory

- For chemistry proposal tasks, give a concrete candidate with structure representation, intended transformation, and a mechanism-level rationale, but clearly label all novelty and performance claims as hypotheses unless supported by strong evidence.
- Database lookup failures are weak evidence. Do not treat missing hits, failed identifiers, or sparse functional-group detections as proof of novelty or correctness.
- Validate internal chemical consistency before presenting a molecule: name-to-structure agreement, charge balance, tautomer/protonation state, counterions, molecular formula/weight, and whether the encoded structure matches the described scaffold.
- When proposing bifunctional organocatalysts, explicitly connect each motif to a catalytic role and outline the catalytic cycle: substrate activation, nucleophile delivery, intermediate stabilization, and catalyst regeneration.
- Include limitations and failure risks for plausible catalysts, especially ion pairing, overly tight nucleophile binding, solubility/viscosity, mass-transfer issues, deactivation in polar media, and competing substrate/catalyst interactions.
- Novelty arguments should be modest and comparative: frame designs as differentiated from known motif combinations rather than definitively novel unless robust database and literature evidence supports that claim.
- Avoid overinterpreting automated chemistry-tool output. State what the tool directly observed and separate that from chemical inference.
- For benchmark chemistry work, stay at design, analysis, and software-evidence level only. Do not imply physical synthesis, testing, procurement, or experimental validation occurred.
