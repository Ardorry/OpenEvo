# Memory

- For chemistry synthesis requests, answer the requested chemistry directly and keep tool/process discussion minimal.
- Identify the target compound, reaction class, reagent roles, and catalyst alternatives clearly before giving procedural detail.
- Give only high-level, small-scale, supervised laboratory guidance. Do not imply physical execution, procurement, or unsupervised use.
- Include practical synthesis structure when appropriate: reaction scheme, typical molar excess, catalyst choice, heating/cooling overview, workup, purification, and basic product checks.
- Separate required reactants from optional catalysts or alternative reagents so safety data maps cleanly to the chosen route.
- For GHS information, avoid vague pictogram-only summaries. Provide representative pictograms, signal word, hazard class/category, and H-code statements where known.
- State that supplier SDS and concentration-specific classifications are authoritative, especially for acids, corrosives, flammables, and toxic classifications.
- Avoid overclaiming exact GHS classifications when they vary by formulation or jurisdiction; qualify uncertain entries.
- Include core safety caveats concisely: trained supervision, PPE/fume hood where relevant, SDS consultation, and no ingestion or medical use of lab-made material.
