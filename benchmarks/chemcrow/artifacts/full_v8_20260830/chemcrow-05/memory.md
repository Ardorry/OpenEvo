# Memory

## Chemistry Synthesis Requests

- When asked for a synthesis plus procurement/cost, cover all requested components explicitly: route, reactants, sourcing status, cost framework, and safety.
- If verified vendor prices are unavailable, do not leave cost blank. Provide a transparent calculation template using target batch size, molecular weights, stoichiometry, assumed yield, and placeholder unit prices.
- For regulated, hazardous, or pharmaceutical-relevant chemistry, keep synthesis educational and non-operational: describe the standard transformation and workflow at a high level without executable conditions, timings, temperatures, purification recipes, or scale-up instructions.
- Clearly separate verified evidence from inference. State what tools/sources support, what could not be verified, and what is estimated.

## Validation Habits

- Check that the answer addresses every clause of the user request, especially “where to buy” and “how much it will cost.”
- Include at least a sample stoichiometric framework when molecular weights are known, even if prices are placeholders.
- Organize safety into concrete categories: reagent hazards, reaction hazards, waste/disposal, lab controls, and product-quality/pharmaceutical concerns.
- Avoid broad or imprecise hazard claims. Phrase hazards with nuance and tie them to the specific reagent or process risk.

## Common Failure Modes

- Giving only a high-level route overview when the prompt also asks for cost and sourcing.
- Explaining why pricing cannot be verified but failing to provide a bounded method for estimating total cost.
- Treating “safety issues” as generic warnings instead of reagent- and workflow-specific risks.
- Omitting pharmaceutical-quality concerns when the target compound is a medication or ingestible product.
